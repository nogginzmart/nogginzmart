Not in a way to like shame others or tell them "erm I'm better than you"--absolutely not. Everyone is in different circumstances & something that may seem easy to me could be a lot more tasking for others. Not to mention any barriers of entry, such as paying for an MP3 player etc etc.

& I am going to say I am pleasantly surprised with how many responses I got! Thank you all that answered! I really appreciate it & you make it possible for me to compile the results like this!

NOW. Why am I getting on this soap box?

I don't know if you'd believe this, but music means a lot to me. A WHOLE lot. So believe me when I say I have tried to listen to music I like in SO many different ways over the years. Even pressing a bad MP3 player up against a TV speaker to record Cannibal by Ke$ha so I can listen to it on the go.

I have also PAID for these platforms to have no ADS, have spent countless hours listening to music on them, & so on & so forth.

but I believe my money, & your money & TIME is better spent ELSEWHERE.

Especially now with people being more & more into diling back on technology & going into things like physical media, I think it's a great time to FIND these alternatives & do research to do it in an affordable way.

For example, my older sister recently asked me to send her a link to the iPod I bought! Which I think is awesome for her to be interested, but I decided against that & went to find her a more afforable & accessible MP3 option.

"But Lee, don't YOU have an iPod you use regularly?"

YES! But I think I can acknowledge that it was NOT a cheap investment. When I got mine is was about ~$175 (USD), looking on Elite Obsolete Electronics (the site I got my iPod), to get a model SIMILAR to the one I have is around or ABOVE $250 (USD). THAT'S INSANE!

Not to mention if you don't like wired headphones & want Bluetooth. Either you go find someone who has put a Bluetooth adapter in the iPod (which will cost extra because that's a custom mod) OR, you do what I did, & buy an expensive adapter that WORKS, yeah, but was ALSO over $100.

SO. Why do I still stand by it? Because I would've been putting THAT MUCH MONEY into a Spotify Premium subscription over time rather than these 1 time purchases!

I hate math, but let me give you an example.

I cancelled my premium subscription around 2024. I was paying monthly, so for an individual account, at that time, that's $10.99/month (USD). That, however, is for a normal user! I was a student, so for cheaper I got the student plan, but I will include both for relation! The student plan cost was $5.99/month (USD) at that time.

for a year on the normal individual, monthly plan, that comes out to $131.88 (USD), NOT including potential taxes

for a year on the student monthly plan, that comes out to $71.88 (USD).

I'm estimating because I do not recall the EXACT price, but for both my bluetooth adaptor & iPod, it would've been around $294 (USD).

294 divided by 5.99 is around 49.08. Rounding that to the whole number & dividing 49 by 12, that would be AROUND 4 years of Spotify Premium. NOT including potential hiking of prices & unavailability of certain types of subscriptions.

If I do this for the STANDARD monthly plan, take 297, divide by 10.99, & it comes out to 27.02. Rounding to the whole number & dividing that by 12 you get 2 & 1/4 of a year on the standard monthly plan. Also not including changes, etc.

I know this is specifically for Spotify, but that's the only one I've had experience with, but this can go for any subscription based model against a 1-time-purchase.

In a world & economy(ies) that have become increasingly more volitile, I think rather than blowing what could be considered chump change one moment but gradually build over time to HUNDREDS, THOUSANDS of dollars--I think you're better off doing the one time purchase for CONTINUED enjoyment & convenience.

Because they have manufactured convenience that WE THINK is convenient IN THE MOMENT. It prays on the shortening attention spans & lack of future sight & entices you to pay. I am taking classes about business & shit--I think you all are smart enough to understand that most of this stuff is predatory to an extent.

WHICH IS WHY I want to try & shorten the journey for you all by PROVIDING resources to get started on your journey to ditch these platforms!

MP3 Players

If you are dead set on getting an iPod, by all means get one. I highly recommend Elite Obsolete Electronics because they refurbish their models & also provide supplementary parts & materials if you want to tinker with your iPod!

Not that platforms like Ebay or any other second hand outlet is bad, but the hardware could have aged overtime & the batteries could be poor, dying screens, & so on could be happening to the device. Not to mention price hiking as this has become more & more popular of an investment. So if you're interested in more cheapter alternatives, don't fear! I've got you!

Innioasis is a great iPod alternative that gives the same vibe while being significantly cheaper! This is the model I recommended to my older sister! It has a USB-C charging port & bluetooth compatibility! & for reference, my iPod has 80 GB os storage. On Amazon specificially, you can get a 128 GB model for just over 80 bucks. That's a steal compared to the 170+ I paid!

If you collect CD's, there's no shortage of CD player's out there! It's almost pretty inexpensive to buy blanks & burn your own CD's! I think the most you'd be paying for is a driver, but the burner I have saved is about ~$20 (USD), so I'd say that's pretty affordable!
